Material Characterization

Using Atomic Force Microscopy we visualize the topography of nanoparticles and by taking force vs distance curves we have determined the Young's modulus of a wide range of nanoparticles: silica-based, polymer-based, lipid-based, and nanocrystals. The integrated AFM-FTIR system is used for determining the chemical composition by getting FTIR spectrum. Read publication here.

My Role: I designed and conducted all the experimental work, data analysis, and manuscript preparation for this project, which is part of the Eli Lilly-Purdue research collaboration.
Outcome: The project led to a publication in Advanced Drug Delivery Reviews and is part of a larger effort to develop advanced drug delivery solutions.
Design Choice: My decisions were aimed at standardizing characterization techniques to ensure consistent and reliable data for the pharmaceutical industry.

Tools used: Cypher S AFM, Cypher ES AFM, MFP 3D Bio AFM, Neaspec NanoFTIR

Acoustics

Developed an acoustic transmitter-receiver system to determine the power delivered and received across different parts of the human arm. Read publication here.

My Role: I was a co-author on the publication. My primary contributions were in the sensor system integration and the use of COMSOL for simulation and data analysis.
Outcome: This project resulted in a publication and successfully demonstrated a method for analyzing acoustic power distribution, which is vital for the development of bio-implants.
Design Choice: We used COMSOL to create a precise simulation of acoustic wave behavior in human tissues, providing a comprehensive understanding of power transfer that would be difficult to measure with physical sensors alone.

Tools used: Sensor system integration, Arduino, COMSOL

My Role: I contributed to the sensor system integration and the computational analysis using COMSOL. I was a co-author on the paper.

Design Choice: The use of both high and low frequencies was a strategic choice to comprehensively analyze the tissue's response and understand the potential for different applications, from drug delivery to imaging.

Tools used: Sensor system integration, Arduino, COMSOL

Fabrication & Materials Engr.

Separate piezoresistive pressure and temperature sensors were fabricated to analyze human body temperature and heart rate. Graphene oxide+PEDOT:PSS was used for the temperature sensor and PDMS+PEDOT:PSS for the pressure sensor. Read publication here.

My Role: I led the fabrication of both the pressure and temperature sensors, and the materials engineering aspect of the project. I also contributed equally to the publication.
Outcome: This project resulted in a functional, wearable sensor system that was published in the IEEE Journal on Flexible Electronics and received a patent.
Design Choice: The selection of Graphene oxide, PEDOT:PSS, and PDMS was based on their piezoresistive properties and biocompatibility, which made them ideal for a flexible, wearable device intended for direct skin contact.

Tools used: Wet lab experience, vacuum oven, materials engineering, chemical analysis

Electrochemical

Developed an electrochromic covalent organic framework thin film and applied it for electronic device safety indication. As voltage changes the thin film color changes and by using the custom-made phone app and clicking a picture of the sensor we can determine the voltage in the system. Read publication here.

My Role: I was a co-inventor of this patented technology. My role focused on the circuit design and the development of the custom Android application for image analysis.
Outcome: This project resulted in a publication in ACS Chemistry of Materials and a patent application, showcasing a new method for visual safety indication in electronics.
Design Choice: The use of a color-changing thin film combined with a smartphone app was a novel approach to create a user-friendly and highly visible safety indicator that did not require a complex display or external power source.

Tools used: Circuit design, Android studio

An electrochemical sensing platform with a specific chemical mixture to accurately determine glucose content in fluids. Read publication here.
